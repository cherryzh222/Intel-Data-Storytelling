Marketing analytics project

Intel Data Storytelling & Visualization

Marketing Analytics · Data Visualization

Analyzed Intel’s 2019–2025 corporate performance and sustainability data, identified five key trends, and developed audience-focused data visualizations and narratives to communicate findings clearly to different stakeholder groups.

Five trends, five audiences

Insight 1 of 5For ESG ratings agencies

Intel now runs on 99% renewable electricity, up 28 points since 2019, even as energy use grew 25%.

Renewable electricity rose from 71% to 99% of global use. Over the same years, energy use grew from 9.6 to 12.0 billion kWh. The clean share grew even as the total did.

Why they care: ratings agencies score the renewable share and whether progress holds as consumption grows.

Chart choice: two small charts, not one dual-axis chart. A line for a share, bars from zero for a total.

Math: 99 − 71 = +28 points · (12.0 − 9.6) ÷ 9.6 = +25.0%

Insight 2 of 5For environmental agencies

Greenhouse gas emissions are 16% below 2019, but have risen 48% since their 2023 low.

Emissions reached a low of 0.89 million metric tons in 2023, then rose to 1.32 in 2025. Energy use also rose over those two years (+11.1%). The data shows that emissions rose, not why.

Why they care: agencies track absolute emissions and whether reductions last.

Chart choice: bars from zero so heights match the values, with the 2023 low and the 2025 value highlighted.

Math: (1.32 − 1.57) ÷ 1.57 = −15.9% · (1.32 − 0.89) ÷ 0.89 = +48.3%

Insight 3 of 5For investors

Revenue is down 26.5% since 2019, but R&D spending held steady, rising from 19% to 26% of revenue.

Net revenue fell from $72.0B to $52.9B while R&D stayed near its 2019 level ($13.4B, then $13.8B), so R&D grew from 18.6% to 26.1% of revenue. Net income went from $21.0B to −$0.3B.

Why they care: investors weigh continued investment in innovation against falling revenue.

Chart choice: revenue as bars from zero, R&D as a share of revenue, because a ratio shows the shift when both numbers move.

Math: (52.9 − 72.0) ÷ 72.0 = −26.5% · 13.4 ÷ 72.0 = 18.6% · 13.8 ÷ 52.9 = 26.1%

Insight 4 of 5For employees

Intel’s workforce is 23% smaller than in 2019, after peaking at 131.9 thousand in 2022.

Employees at year end rose to 131.9 thousand in 2022, then fell to 85.1 thousand in 2025. Capital investments peaked at $25.8B in 2023 and fell to $14.6B. Indexed to 2019, all three lines end below where they started.

Why they care: headcount shapes job security and the outlook for the company.

Chart choice: indexing to 2019 = 100 lets people and dollars share one axis. The dashed line is the 2019 level.

Math: (85.1 − 110.8) ÷ 110.8 = −23.2% · (85.1 − 131.9) ÷ 131.9 = −35.5% · (14.6 − 25.8) ÷ 25.8 = −43.4%

Insight 5 of 5For the general public

Waste is down 36% and water withdrawal is down 12% since 2019, with water progress at 103% net positive.

Waste fell from 387 to 249 thousand short tons, with 3% going to landfill in both years. Water withdrawn dropped from 12.6 to 11.1 billion gallons, and net positive water progress rose from 90% to 103%.

Why they care: less waste and less water taken are easy to grasp without a technical background.

Chart choice: plain bars from zero with the 2019 and 2025 values labeled.

Math: (249 − 387) ÷ 387 = −35.7% · (11.1 − 12.6) ÷ 12.6 = −11.9% · 103 − 90 = +13 points
